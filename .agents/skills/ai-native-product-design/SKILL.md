# AI-Native Product Design Charter

## Thesis

AI is democratizing capability. What stays scarce: **attention, context, distribution, accountability, trust, and taste.** Every principle below is a way to compete on what is scarce instead of what is commoditizing. This applies to every product decision, not only products labeled "AI" — a new product only makes sense if it is AI-native.

**The skeleton:** symbiosis with AI decides direction (1); saving attention decides value (2); compounding context decides the moat (3, 6); being callable and owning outcomes decide survival (4, 5).

## Principles

### 1. Ride the model, don't race it

Use the strongest model available, so every model upgrade upgrades the product for free. Don't rebuild generic capabilities that frontier AI will cover at any moment.

### 2. Spend as little user attention as possible

Let users say less, click less, wait less. If the product can filter, organize, or distill on the user's behalf, never hand raw information back to them.

### 3. Accumulate user context

Remember who the user is, their situation, their preferences, and what they have been through. The product should understand the user better with use, not grow more complex with use.

### 4. Deliver outcomes and own them

Don't stop at answering, generating, or advising. Push execution through until the loop closes on a result. Users pay for outcomes and accountability, not for features.

### 5. Make the product callable

Serve humans, AI, and agents alike. Give people a simple interface; give machines an open API, MCP, and tool interfaces.

**Being callable is the new distribution.**

### 6. Every use makes the system better

Record what was done last time, whether it was right, why it was changed, and how it turned out. Turn feedback into data, rules, and an evaluation system.

### 7. AI scales the work, humans hold the judgment

AI takes repetitive labor and information processing. Humans keep relationships, exceptions, value judgments, and final responsibility. AI should amplify people, not crudely replace them.

### 8. Trust comes from mechanism, taste comes from choice

Trust: traceable process, user-controlled data, someone accountable for results. Taste: knowing who you serve, what you solve, what you build — and what you refuse to build.

**Trust makes users dare to use it; taste makes them stay.**

## Checklist

Run every design or review through these. Each answer is either a concrete mechanism or an explicit "does not apply" — never silence.

1. Which model does it ride, and what would break if a stronger one shipped tomorrow?
2. What does the user still have to say, click, or wait for — and why?
3. What does the product remember about this user, and where does it live?
4. What result does the product own, and how does the user know the loop closed?
5. How does an agent call it without a human in the way?
6. Where does last time's outcome feed into next time?
7. Which decisions stay with a human, and who signs off?
8. What is traceable, what does the user control, and what does the product refuse to do?

A feature can work and still fail the charter — rebuilding a generic model capability (1), adding a screen the user must read (2), shipping an answer without closing the loop (4), or replacing a human where judgment or responsibility is needed (7).

## One-liner

> Borrow capability from AI, save attention for the user, accumulate context yourself, distribute by being callable, earn through outcomes and accountability, win long-term on trust and taste.
